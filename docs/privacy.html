Kaigi Privacy Policy

Kaigi is an open-source plugin for the Omarchy desktop. It runs entirely on your own computer. It has no server, and the author operates no service that receives your data.

Last updated: November 19, 2026

Google user data Kaigi accesses

When you sign in with Google, Kaigi asks for two read-only permissions:

  • View events on all your calendars
    (https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.events.readonly)
  • See the list of Google calendars you're subscribed to
    (https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.calendarlist.readonly)

With them, Kaigi reads:

  • Your calendar list: each calendar's ID, name, and color, and whether it is your primary calendar and whether it is shown (selected) in Google Calendar.
  • Your email address, which is the ID of your primary calendar. Kaigi uses it to label the signed-in account.
  • Events on the calendars you show, from the start of today through the end of tomorrow. Kaigi stores these events as the Google Calendar API returns them: title, start and end time, location, description, conference details, status, organizer, and attendees with their responses (including your own).

Kaigi never creates, changes, or deletes anything in your Google account. It does not create aggregated or anonymized data from your Google data.

How Kaigi uses that data

Kaigi uses Google user data only for features you see on your own desktop:

  • show your next meeting and a countdown in the bar
  • list today's (and optionally tomorrow's) meetings in the popup, dimming past meetings and striking through ones you declined
  • find the meeting link (for example Google Meet or Zoom) and open it when you click it, or at the start time if you turn on auto-join
  • show an event's location, description, and attendees when you open its details in the popup
  • show desktop notifications on your computer when a meeting starts and ends
  • if you turn on hooks, run your own scripts on your computer when a meeting starts and ends, passing them the event's title, start and end time, meeting link, and calendar name
  • let you choose which calendars to show in the settings window
  • open meeting links in the Chrome profile signed in to the same Google account, by matching your email address against Chrome's profile list on your computer

Kaigi does not use Google user data for advertising, profiling, analytics, credit or lending decisions, or any purpose other than these features. Kaigi does not use AI or machine-learning models, and it does not use Google user data to develop, improve, or train any AI or machine-learning model.

Sharing and transfer

Kaigi does not share, transfer, or sell Google user data, raw or aggregated/anonymized, to anyone. That includes the author, advertisers, data brokers, and AI services.

  • Calendar data goes directly from Google's API to your computer and stays there. The author never receives it.
  • Kaigi has no analytics, no telemetry, and no third-party services. Its only network requests related to your Google account go to Google's OAuth and Calendar API endpoints.
  • When you open a meeting, Kaigi passes only that meeting's link to your browser or to the meeting app on your computer.
  • Hooks are off by default. If you turn them on, Kaigi passes the details listed above to scripts you put in ~/.config/omarchy/hooks/ on your own computer. Kaigi itself sends nothing anywhere; what your scripts do is up to you.

How the data is protected

  • All requests to Google use HTTPS.
  • Sign-in uses Google's OAuth 2.0 for installed apps with PKCE and a random state value. The sign-in response is received only on your own computer (127.0.0.1).
  • The sign-in token (OAuth refresh token) is stored in your desktop keyring (GNOME Keyring through the Secret Service API). Only if no keyring is available does it fall back to a file under ~/.local/state/kaigi/ that only your user can read.
  • Short-lived access tokens are kept in memory and never written to disk.
  • Kaigi's cache (~/.cache/kaigi/) and state (~/.local/state/kaigi/) files are created so that only your user account can read them (mode 0600).
  • Kaigi asks only for read-only permissions, and its source code is public so anyone can check what it does.

Retention and deletion

  • Calendar cache: the events and calendar list described above are kept in ~/.cache/kaigi/ so the bar can update without contacting Google every minute. The cache covers only today and tomorrow, and Kaigi replaces it on every refresh (every 5 minutes by default). It stays on your computer until you sign out or delete it.
  • Account details: your email address and the refresh token are kept until you sign out.
  • Sign out: the "Sign out" button in Kaigi, or bin/kaigi-google logout, revokes the token with Google, deletes it from the keyring, removes the account from Kaigi's settings, and deletes that account's calendar cache.
  • Revoke access from Google's side: you can revoke Kaigi's access at any time at https://myaccount.google.com/permissions. Kaigi then can no longer read your calendar. To also remove data already cached on your computer, sign out in Kaigi or delete the folders below.
  • Uninstall: deleting ~/.config/kaigi, ~/.local/state/kaigi, and ~/.cache/kaigi removes everything Kaigi stored.

Because the author never receives your data, there is nothing stored elsewhere to delete.

Use of Google user data

Kaigi's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.
